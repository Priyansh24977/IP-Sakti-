The Basmati Rice Patent Case (US Patent 5,663,484) - RiceTec:

Background: On September 2, 1997, the USPTO granted US Patent 5,663,484, titled "Basmati Rice Lines and Grains," to RiceTec Inc., a Texas-based company. The patent covered rice lines developed by crossing traditional Basmati varieties from India and Pakistan with American long-grain and semi-dwarf rice varieties, and included 20 claims covering aspects of the resulting hybrid rice lines, plants, and grains, marketed under names including Texmati, Jasmati, and Kasmati.

The Challenge: Indian farmers and advocacy groups raised strong objections when the patent was granted, arguing it amounted to biopiracy - RiceTec's hybrid was developed using the genetic heritage of Basmati varieties bred and cultivated over centuries by farmers in India and Pakistan. India's Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority (APEDA) formally filed a re-examination request with the USPTO on April 28, 2000. Dr. Vandana Shiva's Research Foundation for Science, Technology and Ecology also pursued public interest litigation in India's Supreme Court in 1998, seeking to compel the Indian government to challenge the patent.

Outcome - Progressive Claim Reductions: The case did not end in a single decisive revocation like the turmeric or Neem cases, but instead saw RiceTec's claims progressively narrowed over several years:
- In June 2000, RiceTec voluntarily withdrew 4 of its 20 original claims (relating to grain chalkiness characteristics).
- In 2001, following the re-examination process, a USPTO re-examiner rejected 13 of the remaining 16 claims, finding that the rice lines, plants, and grains covered by those claims were substantially identical to, or anticipated by, existing Basmati varieties already grown in India and Pakistan and therefore not patentable.
- By 2002, as a result of continued judicial and political challenges, RiceTec had withdrawn or lost a total of 15 of its original 20 claims. The USPTO also ordered that the patent's title be changed from "Basmati Rice Lines and Grains" to "Rice Lines Bas867, RT1117 and RT1121," removing the word "Basmati" from the patent's official designation.

Remaining claims: A small number of claims covering RiceTec's specific hybrid rice lines (not the term "Basmati" itself, nor traditional Basmati varieties) survived, since RiceTec's actual hybrid breeding work was found to have some genuinely distinguishing characteristics, even though its broader claims over "Basmati" rice generally were rejected.

Why this case is different from turmeric and Neem: Unlike the turmeric and Neem cases, which ended in full revocation, the Basmati case is better understood as a partial victory through incremental claim narrowing rather than a single clean outcome. It illustrates that biopiracy-related patent challenges do not always result in complete cancellation - sometimes the more realistic outcome is significantly narrowing an overly broad patent to remove claims over traditional/prior-known material while leaving narrower, genuinely novel claims intact. This case also directly motivated India's subsequent introduction of Geographical Indication (GI) legislation, partly to provide a more durable protection mechanism for regionally-linked agricultural products like Basmati rice, going forward.
