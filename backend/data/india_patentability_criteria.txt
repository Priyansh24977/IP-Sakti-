Patentability Criteria under the Patents Act, 1970 (India):

For an invention to be patentable in India, it must satisfy three core requirements:

1. Novelty: The invention must be new and must not have been disclosed in the public domain anywhere in the world before the filing date. This is assessed against "prior art" - existing knowledge, patents, or publications.

2. Inventive Step (Non-Obviousness): The invention must involve a technical advance compared to existing knowledge, or have economic significance, and must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant field.

3. Industrial Applicability: The invention must be capable of being made or used in an industry, meaning it should have practical utility and not be purely theoretical.

Additionally, under Section 3, several categories are excluded from patentability regardless of meeting the above criteria, including: traditional knowledge (Section 3p), methods of agriculture or horticulture, medical treatment methods for humans or animals, and mere discovery of a new form of a known substance without enhancement of known efficacy.

For Ayurvedic and herbal products specifically, a genuinely new drug delivery system, a novel extraction or purification process, or a new combination showing unexpected synergistic effects may be patentable, even if the underlying plant or ingredient itself is traditionally known.
