India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002 - Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS):

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 governs access to India's biological resources and associated traditional knowledge, and establishes the Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) framework that requires fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of these resources.

Key institutions: The Act establishes a three-tier structure - the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at the national level, State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) at the state level, and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at the local/panchayat level.

Approval requirement: Under Section 3 of the Act, any person seeking to obtain biological resources from India for research, commercial use, or bio-survey/bio-utilization purposes must obtain prior approval from the NBA. This applies particularly to foreign entities, non-resident Indians, and companies with non-Indian participation. Indian citizens and companies generally need to only notify the relevant State Biodiversity Board, though this notification requirement can vary based on the nature of use.

Benefit sharing mechanism: When a biological resource or associated traditional knowledge is used for commercial purposes (including patents), the Act mandates that the benefits arising from that use be shared with the benefit claimers - which may be individuals, communities, or the government, depending on whether the traditional knowledge can be traced to a specific identifiable group.

Connection to patents: Under Section 6 of the Act, anyone applying for a patent (in India or abroad) based on biological material obtained from India must obtain prior approval from the NBA before the patent is granted. This creates a direct link between IP filing and ABS compliance for any Ayurvedic product whose active ingredients are sourced from Indian biological resources.

2023 Amendment: The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 introduced changes aimed at easing compliance for the AYUSH sector, including simplified registration processes for practitioners of Indian systems of medicine and codified traditional knowledge, and adjustments to benefit-sharing obligations for certain categories of users. The exact simplified provisions and thresholds should be verified against the current 2024 Rules before citing specific compliance thresholds in a demo.

Practical relevance for Ayurveda IP: A company developing a new Ayurvedic formulation using a specific plant sourced from Indian forests or biodiversity-rich regions needs BOTH: (1) patent clearance under Section 3(p) analysis (is this genuinely novel or traditional knowledge), and (2) separate ABS approval under the Biological Diversity Act if the biological resource itself was accessed from India. These are two distinct compliance requirements that often get confused as being the same thing.
